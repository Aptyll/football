Three mobile directions for the football game

Each direction is drawn as three phone screens: the "home" screen, the moment of calling a play, and the payoff. The football rules stay the same in all three. What changes is how it looks, how it feels in the hand, and what brings her back tomorrow. Her team is plain crimson and white with no logos or real names.

One clear goal, always visibleCandy Crush is criticized when the goal hides behind an icon. Show "Score a TD, 2:30 left" in one fixed spot.
Big buttons in the thumb zoneBlock Blast's controls are "palm-sized." All play choices sit in the bottom 40% of the screen, at least 76px tall.
Fancy font for titles onlyDisplay fonts hurt reading. Headlines can be bubbly; play names and explanations use a plain, heavy font at 17px or more.
A "wow" payoffBig cascades are King's designed "wow" moments. A touchdown should earn a full-screen celebration, stars, and confetti.
Short sessions, a reason to returnOne drive is about 2 minutes, a perfect puzzle-game session. A season map, a daily drive, or a sticker album gives the "one more" pull.
Helpers should feel fairBoosters work when players can see they're the right tool. Football already has them: timeouts, a coach's hint, the hurry-up.

A. Season Trail

The closest to Candy Crush. Her season is a winding path of games, each drive earns up to three stars, and the controls are glossy, candy-colored buttons colored by play type.

  • Stars give every drive a grade: 1 for scoring, 2 for scoring with time left, 3 for no turnovers and a timeout saved.
  • Timeouts, Coach's Hint and Hurry-up act as football "boosters" and tie into the clock work already planned.
  • Risk: the most "kiddie" look of the three, and the most art to build.

B. Playbook Blocks

The closest to Block Blast. A calm, dark board with bright flat tiles. The field is a grid that fills up as she gains yards, and each first down "clears a row" like a line clear.

  • Like Block Blast's three pieces, she's dealt 3 of the 6 plays each down. This makes every call a small puzzle (a rule change, optional).
  • Combos for chained first downs and a high-score "best drive" give replay value without a season to build.
  • Cheapest to build and the easiest to read. Risk: least "football gameday" feeling of the three.

C. Gameday Scrapbook

Cozier and more grown-up, like the newer word and decorating puzzle games. Warm paper, a houndstooth trim, a chalkboard play diagram, and plays dealt as index cards. Big moments earn stickers for a season album.

  • Feels like a coach's notebook, which suits a longtime fan who knows the game.
  • The sticker album ("Goal Line Stand", "Two-Minute Drill") rewards specific feats, so she collects stories instead of points.
  • Risk: only two index cards fit on screen, so she swipes to see the rest. That is one more step than A or B.
